# Public research source review: crowd motion annotations

**Reviewed 2026-11-18.** This is a date-bounded review of public source descriptions and access. It is not an experiment protocol or result. No motion-accuracy or warning-effectiveness evaluation ran, and no source media or annotations are redistributed here.

## Source comparison

No current primary route provides that pair within the review cap. Track-box centers are not dense optical-flow ground truth. |
| [HUM-CARD, "A human crowded annotated real dataset"](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.is.2024.102409), [author-hosted paper](https://iris.enea.it/retrieve/0955437c-98ff-4c5d-9975-fe29c020ff24/HUM-CARD_%20A%20human%20crowded%20annotated%20real%20dataset.pdf) | The paper describes manual boxes for agents in each annotated frame, nine expert annotators, and about 5,000 hours of labeling effort. Experts double-blind checked doubtful cases; the paper does not report an agreement statistic or a general independent double-coding protocol. It says labeling is ongoing and some agents were temporarily omitted. It does not specify interpolation rules. "Physical interaction" is a contact-behavior category, not a force, harm, crush, or adverse-event label. | The publisher says the data will be shared at a link in the paper after acceptance, but the reviewed article does not identify an operational annotation download. No annotation file was accessed. CC BY 4.0 applies to the article, not automatically to its underlying YouTube, UCY, or Grand Central source videos. Credit Di Gennaro et al. for the paper; underlying video and annotation reuse terms remain unverified. | Useful for a descriptive comparison of behavior labels and ontology. It cannot currently provide time-aligned reference motion or harm outcomes for this project. The article's "physical interaction" wording does not establish physical force. |

Across these sources, a person's annotated box center is a sparse image-space reference for that person. It is not dense flow truth for the surrounding image patch. Interpolated boxes must not be counted as independent manual observations. None of the reviewed labels is an independently adjudicated crowd-crush outcome set. These sources do not establish calibrated force, physical compression, warning thresholds, or warning effectiveness.

## Synthetic optical-flow data

[TUB CrowdFlow](https://github.com/tsenst/CrowdFlow), described by Schröder, Senst, Bochinski, and Sikora in the [AVSS 2018 paper](https://elvera.nue.tu-berlin.de/files/1548Schr%C3%B6der2018.pdf), contains ten rendered sequences across five scenes, with static and moving-camera variants and author-provided flow fields and trajectories. The repository README describes the evaluation framework as MIT-licensed and separately restricts dataset use to academic purposes. These are CrowdFlow terms; this project's MIT code license does not grant rights to CrowdFlow data. Because its scenes are rendered, CrowdFlow may help check software behavior against synthetic flow labels; it cannot establish accuracy on real crowd video or replace real-data evidence.

## Access decision

CroHD has a current official release, but no bounded annotation/image pair has passed file-level review. UCY's linked archive exceeds the bounded review size and its page gives no reuse terms. SDD's official archive is about 69 GB, with no selective primary download route identified in the reviewed materials. HUM-CARD's paper does not expose the promised annotation files. These are access findings from the sources reviewed on the date above, not claims that the datasets are unavailable everywhere.
