# Research references and attribution

These works motivated research questions in this repository. They are not evidence that the code reproduces their methods, measures their quantities, or has empirical effectiveness. The MIT licence covers original code here only. Each paper, dataset, video, and other source retains its own copyright and reuse terms.

## Crowd mechanisms and collective motion

- Rob J. Hyndman and George Athanasopoulos. *Forecasting: Principles and Practice*, 3rd ed. [Official book site](https://otexts.com/fpp3/), including the [forecasting toolbox](https://otexts.com/fpp3/toolbox.html) and [ARIMA models](https://otexts.com/fpp3/arima.html). General reference for persistence, trend, and autoregressive concepts. This repository's implementation is not a package or exact replication of the book.
- E. S. Page (1954). "Continuous Inspection Schemes." *Biometrika* 41(1-2), 100-115. [DOI: 10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100). This is a reference for the diagnostic CUSUM family used by the baseline module. The implementation is not a calibrated replication of Page's scheme.
- Gideon Schwarz (1978). "Estimating the Dimension of a Model." *The Annals of Statistics* 6(2), 461-464. [Institute of Mathematical Statistics publisher DOI: 10.1214/aos/1176344136](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176344136). Cited only as penalty inspiration for the one-split exploratory density heuristic, not as a reproduction of Schwarz's method.
- Dirk Helbing, Anders Johansson, and Habib Zein Al-Abideen (2007). “Dynamics of crowd disasters: An empirical study.” *Physical Review E* 75, 046109. [DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevE.75.046109](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.75.046109). The Mina video analysis motivates studying transitions in collective motion. Its local density times spatially local velocity variance is not the temporal zone-average variance used by this prototype, and it is not direct mechanical pressure.
- François Gu, Benjamin Guiselin, Nicolas Bain, Iker Zuriguel, and Denis Bartolo (2025). “Emergence of collective oscillations in massive human crowds.” *Nature* 638, 112–119. [DOI: 10.1038/s41586-024-08514-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-08514-6). This work motivates questions about coherent oscillation and confinement. It does not validate a general warning signal.
- Arianna Bottinelli and Jesse L. Silverberg (2018). “Can high-density human collective motion be forecasted by spatiotemporal fluctuations?” [arXiv:1809.07875](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07875), [DOI: 10.48550/arXiv.1809.07875](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1809.07875). Their roughly one-second density-wave forecast in a particular Oasis concert clip is limited evidence for that setting, not a general crush-warning result.
- Sina Feldmann and Juliane Adrian (2023). “Forward propagation of a push through a row of people.” *Safety Science* 164, 106173. [DOI: 10.1016/j.ssci.2023.106173](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssci.2023.106173), [JuSER record](https://juser.fz-juelich.de/record/1007496). A controlled row study informs propagation questions, not dense-event warning effectiveness.
- Sina Feldmann, Thomas Chatagnon, Juliane Adrian, Julien Pettré, and Armin Seyfried (2024). “Temporal segmentation of motion propagation in response to an external impulse.” *Safety Science* 175, 106512. [DOI: 10.1016/j.ssci.2024.106512](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssci.2024.106512). This five-person-row study motivates questions about impulse phases and observability; it is not a dense-event warning evaluation.
- Sina Feldmann, Juliane Adrian, and Maik Boltes (2024). “Propagation of controlled frontward impulses through standing crowds.” *Collective Dynamics* 9, 1–16. [DOI: 10.17815/CD.2024.148](https://doi.org/10.17815/CD.2024.148), [arXiv:2503.19110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19110) (submitted 2025). The paper reports controlled variation in push intensity, spacing, preparation, and formation. These staged conditions do not validate warning in natural crowd events.
- Dirk Helbing, Anders Johansson, and Habib Z. Al-Abideen (2007). “Crowd turbulence: The physics of crowd disasters.” [arXiv:0708.3339](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.3339). Related work by the same authors, cited separately from the empirical study.
- Jesse L. Silverberg, Matthew Bierbaum, James P. Sethna, and Itai Cohen (2013). “Collective Motion of Humans in Mosh and Circle Pits at Heavy Metal Concerts.” *Physical Review Letters* 110, 228701. [DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevLett.110.228701](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.110.228701). This work motivates treating intentional concert motion as a competing explanation for apparent disorder or oscillation.

CORDIS lists laboratory and field datasets and research demonstrators. Listing does not establish current suitability or warning effectiveness.
- CrowdDNA official project website: [crowddna.eu](https://crowddna.eu/). The European Commission CORDIS record is the project reference used here.

This account describes the research programme; it is not an independent prospective evaluation.

## Data records and sensing methods

- François Gu, Benjamin Guiselin, and Denis Bartolo. “Emergence of collective oscillations in massive human crowds. Video, figures data and simulations code. Release for Nature.” Zenodo record 14050598 (2025). [DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.14050598](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.14050598), [record API](https://zenodo.org/api/records/14050598). These three people are the dataset-record authors. The related Nature paper has five authors, listed separately above. The original selected subset is five files totalling 4,697,499 bytes; a selected 51,275,828-byte video is admitted separately. These selected files are not the full 1.22 GB record. The density table records author-declared seconds relative to festival opening and spatially averaged density in m^-2. It contains no velocity or outcome labels. The record declares record-level CC BY 4.0 and supplies the input for the [descriptive density forecast benchmark](results/pamplona-density-30s-2026-11-18/README.md) and the [exploratory change-point forecast](results/pamplona-change-point-2026-11-18/README.md). See [sources/pamplona-video.json](sources/pamplona-video.json) for the selected video URL, checksums, and retrieval metadata.
- Oscar Dufour et al. “Dense Crowd Dynamics and Pedestrian Trajectories: A Multiscale Field Dataset from the Festival of Lights in Lyon.” *Scientific Data* 12, 718 (2025). [DOI: 10.1038/s41597-025-04732-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-04732-3). The related archive record is “Dense Crowd Dynamics and Pedestrian Trajectories: A Multiscale Field Study at the Fête des Lumières in Lyon,” Zenodo record 13830435 (2024). [DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.13830435](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.13830435). The selected 33,639,872-byte ZIP is a separate archive-level admission, not the full record inventory. The Zenodo record declares CC BY 4.0 at record scope. The Python reader covers one TopView text member. See [sources/lyon-topview.json](sources/lyon-topview.json) for the selected archive URL, checksums, and retrieval metadata.
- “Dataset: Impulse propagation through a small standing crowd.” [JuSER record, DOI 10.34735/ped.2022.6](https://doi.org/10.34735/ped.2022.6); [archive homepage and research-use terms](https://ped.fz-juelich.de/da/doku.php?id=start). The homepage permits research with attribution but does not grant redistribution. A bounded source audit inspected the 260,536-byte metadata file and 12,855,563-byte pressure ZIP, then read one matching member (1,109,247 uncompressed bytes) containing 100 50×50 matrices. This was source inspection only. Response extraction was **NOT RUN**; matrix units, pressure calibration, and pressure-to-camera mapping remain unverified. No source payload is redistributed.
- European Commission CORDIS, CrowdDNA dataset result listings, above. The separate selected Zenodo video is documented in its manifest; other CORDIS formats, labels, availability, and dataset-specific terms remain unverified.
- Shi et al. (2019). “Revisiting Perspective Information for Efficient Crowd Counting.” *Proceedings of CVPR 2019*. [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2019/papers/Shi_Revisiting_Perspective_Information_for_Efficient_Crowd_Counting_CVPR_2019_paper.pdf). Perspective and scene assumptions motivate camera-calibration cautions, not a validated measurement pipeline here.
- Cong et al. (2022). “STCrowd: A Multimodal Dataset for Pedestrian Perception in Crowded Scenes.” *Proceedings of CVPR 2022*. [CVF paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Cong_STCrowd_A_Multimodal_Dataset_for_Pedestrian_Perception_in_Crowded_Scenes_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf). It is relevant to multimodal sensing and occlusion, not crowd-crush outcomes.

## Interpretation

The references motivate measurement and model questions. None demonstrates the effectiveness of this repository's implementation. In particular, published use of terms such as “pressure,” “turbulence,” or “risk” does not make this project's motion-derived values measurements of force or calibrated safety probabilities.
